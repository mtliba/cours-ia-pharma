In-context learning

Adaptation without changing any weight.

A few solved examples, written into the model's input.

The case to predict, appended after them.

The weights. They are not updated: all the adaptation happens in the forward pass.

The examples are data for the forward pass, not for gradient descent.

Session 9 of 10 · Foundation models and adaptation

[1 click] In-context learning deserves its own slide because it is conceptually new. The model receives, in its input, a few solved examples followed by a new case, and predicts the answer for the new case. No weight changes. The learning algorithm is executed by the forward pass. For language models this is few-shot prompting. TabPFN turns the idea into a tabular method: the whole training table is given as context, and the prediction for a new row takes one forward pass. On datasets of up to ten thousand samples its authors report that it outperforms tuned gradient-boosted trees. For small clinical tables it is now a baseline you should run.
