τ = 0.05: the positive gets 0.98, the hard negative 0.02. Only the hard negative produces gradient.

τ = 1: the positive gets only 0.30; all negatives count almost equally.

Small τ: sharper separation, but similar examples are torn apart.

Typical values: 0.05 to 0.2. Treat it as a hyperparameter of first importance.

Session 7 of 10 · Representation learning and self-supervision

The temperature is not a detail. Here are the softmax probabilities for one positive, with similarity zero point nine, and five negatives, for three temperatures. In blue, tau equals zero point zero five: the positive gets 0.98 and almost all the rest goes to the hardest negative, the one with similarity zero point seven. The gradient comes from hard negatives only. In grey, tau equals one: the distribution is nearly flat, the positive gets 0.30, and every negative counts about the same. A low temperature separates fine-grained differences, but it also pushes apart examples that truly are similar, two patients with the same phenotype for instance. Typical values lie between zero point zero five and zero point two.
