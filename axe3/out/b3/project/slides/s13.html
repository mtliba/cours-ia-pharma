VICReg: each requirement as one term

The clearest statement of what a joint-embedding method needs.

The batch of embeddings for the first and second views.

Invariance: mean squared distance between the two views of each example.

Variance: keeps the standard deviation of every dimension, across the batch, above a threshold. This term forbids collapse.

Covariance: penalizes correlations between dimensions, so that they carry different information.

Invariance alone collapses. Variance prevents it. Covariance spreads information across dimensions.

[1 click] VICReg is worth knowing because it states the requirements one by one. Z and Z prime are the embeddings of the two views for a whole batch. The first term, s, is invariance: the two views of each example should match. Alone, it leads to collapse. The second term, v, is variance: each dimension of the embedding must keep a standard deviation above a threshold across the batch. A constant embedding has zero variance, so this term forbids collapse. The third term, c, penalizes covariance between dimensions, so that the dimensions do not all encode the same thing. This last failure, where the embedding uses only a few directions, is called dimensional collapse.
