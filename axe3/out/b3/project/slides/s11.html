[1 click] A useful way to understand the contrastive loss, due to Wang and Isola. It does two things. Alignment: the two views of an example should land close to each other; the first line is their mean squared distance. Uniformity: embeddings of different examples should spread evenly over the sphere; the second line is small when points are far apart. On the left, a collapsed representation: perfectly aligned, not uniform at all, and useless. On the right, what we want. These two numbers can be computed on any encoder and are good diagnostics during pre-training, long before you have a downstream result.
