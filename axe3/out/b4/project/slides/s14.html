Training and sampling

A regression loss; then an iterative sampler.

The network: given a noisy sample and the step, it predicts the noise that was added.

Average over data points, noise draws and steps chosen at random.

Fresh Gaussian noise re-injected at each sampling step, except the last.

Training is plain regression. The cost is paid at sampling time.

Session 8 of 10 · Deep generative models

[1 click] Training, first line. Take a data point, pick a step t at random, draw a noise epsilon, build x t with the closed form, and ask the network to predict epsilon. The loss is a mean squared error. That is all: a regression problem, stable to train, with no adversary and no posterior collapse. It can be derived as a weighted variational bound, but the simple form works best. Sampling, second line. Start from pure noise at step T. At each step, subtract the predicted noise, suitably scaled, and add a little fresh noise. Repeat down to step zero. The price is the number of network evaluations: hundreds originally, tens with modern samplers.
