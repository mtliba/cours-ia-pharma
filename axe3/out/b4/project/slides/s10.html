Session 8 of 10 · Deep generative models

[1 click] One technical point makes the whole thing trainable. The bound contains an expectation over codes sampled from the encoder, and you cannot differentiate through a sampling operation. The trick: write the sample as the mean plus the standard deviation times a noise variable epsilon drawn from a standard Gaussian. The randomness now sits in epsilon, which does not depend on the parameters, and z is a differentiable function of the encoder outputs. Why do chemists like these models? The latent space is smooth and continuous. One can take a molecule's code, move in the direction that improves a predicted property, and decode: optimization in a continuous space instead of a search over discrete structures.
