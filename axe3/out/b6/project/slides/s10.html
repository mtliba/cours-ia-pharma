Three ways to combine mechanism and learning

From the most to the least constrained.

1. Learn the parameters

A network maps a patient's covariates to individual CL, V and k_a; the differential equation does the rest. Predictions obey mass balance and extrapolate to new doses.

Deep compartment models (Janssen et al., 2022).

2. Learn a correction

Keep the mechanistic equation and add a neural term for what it misses. The network explains only the residual.

Universal differential equations (Rackauckas et al., 2020).

3. Learn from simulations

Use a published population model to simulate thousands of profiles, and train a flexible model on them; then test on real patients.

Woillard et al., 2021.

The less data you have, the more structure you should impose.

Session 10 of 10 · Multimodal and longitudinal health data: from models to decisions

[1 click] There are three ways to combine the two. One: learn the parameters. A network maps a patient's covariates to individual clearance, volume and absorption rate, and the differential equation produces the concentration curve. The prediction obeys mass balance by construction, and it extrapolates to a dose never seen. Two: learn a correction. Keep the mechanistic equation and add a neural term for what it misses; the network only has to explain the residual. Three: learn from simulations. Take a published population model, simulate thousands of rich profiles, train a flexible model on them, and test on real patients. The choice depends on how much you trust the mechanistic model, and on how much data you have.
