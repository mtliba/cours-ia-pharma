Session 10 of 10 · Multimodal and longitudinal health data: from models to decisions

Decision-curve analysis gives one number for usefulness. Choose a risk threshold, p t, above which you would act; say twenty percent. That choice encodes a trade-off: at twenty percent you accept four false alarms for one true case. The net benefit is the proportion of true positives minus the proportion of false positives weighted by that exchange rate. It is expressed in true positives per patient. Then compare with two strategies that need no model: act on everyone, and act on no one, whose net benefit is zero. A model is useful at a threshold only if it beats both. Plot this across thresholds and you have a decision curve.
