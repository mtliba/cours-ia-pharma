Time-to-event outcomes

Graft loss is not a yes-or-no label: follow-up may end first.

The time of the event, in discrete intervals (months, for instance).

The hazard: the probability that the event occurs in interval t, given that it has not occurred before.

1 if the event was observed for patient i; 0 if follow-up ended first (censoring).

Every interval survived contributes to the likelihood, for censored patients too.

Session 10 of 10 · Multimodal and longitudinal health data: from models to decisions

Many clinical outcomes are times to an event, and for many patients the event has not happened when follow-up ends: they are censored. Treating them as negatives is wrong. A simple and effective approach uses discrete time. The network outputs a hazard for each interval: the probability that the event occurs in that interval given that it has not occurred before. The likelihood has two parts. For every interval a patient survived, the log of one minus the hazard. And if the event was observed, delta equals one, the log of the hazard at the event time. Censored patients contribute everything we know: that they survived until they left. Evaluate with a concordance index and time-dependent calibration.
