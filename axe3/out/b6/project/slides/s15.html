Session 10 of 10 · Multimodal and longitudinal health data: from models to decisions

[1 click] Now the delicate step. A dosing decision needs to know what would happen if the dose were changed, compared with not changing it. Formally: the difference between two potential outcomes for the same patient, Y of one and Y of zero. Only one of them is ever observed. A predictive model estimates something else: the expected outcome given the characteristics and the dose that was actually chosen, under the prescribing habits that generated the data. The diagram shows the problem. The last level and renal function influence the dose the clinician chose, and they also influence the outcome. A model will find that high doses go with poor outcomes, simply because sicker patients received higher doses.
