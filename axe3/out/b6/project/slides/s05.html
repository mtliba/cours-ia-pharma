Session 10 of 10 · Multimodal and longitudinal health data: from models to decisions

Five properties. Irregular sampling: visits are not equally spaced. Informative missingness: a test is ordered because a clinician is worried, so the mere fact that creatinine was measured three times in a week carries information, and it also biases any naive analysis. Several modalities at different rates. Treatment feedback: the dose at one visit was chosen in response to the level at the previous one, so dose and outcome are entangled. And censoring and shift: follow-up ends, patients move, assays change. Each property has a modelling consequence, in the right column. The rest of the session takes them in turn.
