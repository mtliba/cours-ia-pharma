Adam and AdamW

A separate step size for each parameter.

Running mean of the gradient: momentum. Default β₁ = 0.9.

Running mean of the squared gradient, element by element. Default β₂ = 0.999.

Bias-corrected versions, m divided by (1 − β₁ᵗ): they compensate for starting at zero.

A small constant (10⁻⁸) that avoids division by zero.

Decoupled weight decay: shrinks the weights directly. This term is what makes it AdamW.

Default for transformers: AdamW with warm-up and cosine decay.

Session 5 of 10 · Optimization and generalization in deep networks

[1 click] Adam keeps two running averages for every parameter. m is the average gradient, as in momentum. v is the average squared gradient. The update divides m by the square root of v: parameters with consistently large gradients take smaller steps, parameters with small gradients take larger ones. In effect each parameter gets its own learning rate. The hats are bias corrections for the first steps, when the averages are still close to their zero initialization. The last term is weight decay applied directly to the weights, outside the adaptive rescaling: that is AdamW. In the original Adam, the decay was added to the gradient and then rescaled, which made the regularization uneven.
