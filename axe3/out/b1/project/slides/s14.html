The residual block

The unit of every modern architecture.

The representation entering block l.

What the block learns: a correction (attention, convolution or MLP).

Normalization applied before the block: the pre-norm arrangement used in current transformers.

The identity term: whatever F does, a gradient path of gain 1 reaches every earlier layer.

Stacks of pre-norm residual blocks: that is what a transformer is.

Session 5 of 10 · Optimization and generalization in deep networks

[1 click] A residual block adds a learned correction to its input. First line: x of layer l plus one equals x of layer l, plus F of the normalized input. Unroll this across layers and differentiate: second line. The derivative of the last representation with respect to an early one is the identity plus other terms. So whatever the blocks do, there is a path of gain one from the loss to every layer. Gradients cannot vanish through that path. That is why we can train networks with hundreds of layers, and why every architecture in the next sessions, transformers included, is a stack of residual blocks.
