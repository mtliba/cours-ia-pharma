A loss is a negative log-likelihood

Mean squared error and cross-entropy are two cases of the same principle.

A Gaussian density for the target, centred on the network output. Its negative log is the squared error: MSE assumes additive noise of constant variance.

A categorical distribution over classes. Its negative log is the cross-entropy.

Turns the network outputs (logits) into probabilities that sum to one.

1 for the true class c, 0 otherwise (one-hot target).

Choosing a loss is choosing a noise model. For concentrations, think proportional error.

[1 click] Where do losses come from? From likelihoods. Assume the target is the network output plus Gaussian noise of constant variance. The negative log-density is the squared error divided by two sigma squared, plus a constant: minimizing the mean squared error is maximum likelihood under that noise model. Assume instead a categorical distribution over classes, with probabilities given by a softmax of the network outputs. The negative log-likelihood is the cross-entropy. This is practical. Drug concentrations usually have proportional error, larger at high concentrations. Plain MSE is then the wrong noise model: train on log-concentrations, or let the network predict the variance as well.
