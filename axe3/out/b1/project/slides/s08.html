Stochastic gradient descent with momentum

A noisy gradient from a minibatch, averaged over time.

The minibatch drawn at step t: a few dozen to a few thousand examples.

The minibatch gradient: an unbiased but noisy estimate of the full gradient.

The learning rate: the step size.

The momentum coefficient (typically 0.9) and the velocity: a running sum of past gradients.

Gradient noise scales with η / |B|: change the batch size and the learning rate together.

Session 5 of 10 · Optimization and generalization in deep networks

[1 click] Computing the gradient on the whole dataset at each step is too expensive. We draw a minibatch, B t, and average the gradients over it. That gives g t: unbiased, but noisy. The noise is useful, it helps escape sharp regions, and its scale grows with the ratio of learning rate to batch size. That is why, when you multiply the batch size by k, you usually multiply the learning rate by about k as well. Second line: momentum. We keep a velocity v, a running sum of past gradients with decay mu, typically zero point nine, and we step along the velocity. Directions that agree across steps accumulate; directions that oscillate cancel out.
