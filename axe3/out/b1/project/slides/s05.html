Learning as empirical risk minimization

The one formula behind every model in this course.

The network, a function with parameters θ (all weights and biases).

The per-example loss: how wrong the prediction is for example i.

The empirical risk: the average loss over the n training examples. It estimates the expected loss on the whole population.

The regularizer, for instance the squared norm of the weights, with strength λ.

The parameters we keep: an approximate minimizer found by gradient descent.

We minimize the empirical risk, but we care about the population risk.

Two honest remarks: the problem is non-convex, so we find a good solution rather than the minimum; and the gap between empirical and population risk is exactly the generalization gap from session three.
